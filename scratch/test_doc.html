Abstract

In modern higher educational institutions, the efficient tracking, safeguarding, and accounting of capital assets are vital to institutional governance, financial compliance, and operational sustainability. Debre Berhan University (DBU), an expansive higher educational establishment comprising multiple colleges, faculties, administrative directorates, and specialized research laboratories, historically managed its physical assets through fragmented manual ledgers, paper-based issue vouchers, and disconnected spreadsheet records. These conventional workflows suffered from critical operational vulnerabilities, including lack of real-time custodian accountability, pervasive discrepancies during annual physical audits, delayed maintenance interventions, untracked capital depreciation, and complete absence of instant public verification.

To resolve these institutional bottlenecks, this project designed, engineered, implemented, and empirically verified the DBU Asset Tracking System—a centralized, enterprise-grade, web-based property administration platform. Developed on a modern three-tier architecture utilizing Next.js 14 (App Router, Server Actions, and React Server Components), TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Prisma ORM, and a cloud-native PostgreSQL database hosted on Neon, the system introduces a deterministic state-machine workflow across the entire asset lifecycle.

Key engineered innovations include: (1) a Two-Phase Custody Handshake Engine requiring staff members to formally accept or reject asset assignments with mandatory audit justification; (2) an automated QR Code and Code 39 Barcode Engine generating unique printable labels and embedding non-guessable, privacy-safe publicId UUIDs; (3) a Zero-Authentication Public QR Verification Interface that enables security personnel and auditors to verify authentic university asset registration while cryptographically masking personal custodian identities and strictly suppressing sensitive financial or procurement records; (4) a centralized Maintenance and Defect Triage Subsystem that automatically generates repair tickets upon damaged returns; (5) a Physical Inventory Verification Subsystem facilitating camera-based scanning, anti-tamper duplicate prevention, and cross-department mismatch detection; and (6) a mathematically rigorous Straight-Line Depreciation and Financial Valuation Engine calculating elapsed amortization, carrying book values, salvage value thresholds, and operational replacement scores in real time.

Comprehensive automated integration tests, live server RBAC tests, and empirical database audits validated 100% test pass rates across all 7 authenticated user roles and public endpoints. The resulting system establishes transparent property governance, eliminates ghost assets, protects university capital, and provides an extensible blueprint for Ethiopian higher education digitalization.

Keywords: Asset Tracking, Debre Berhan University, Next.js 14, Prisma ORM, PostgreSQL, QR Code Verification, Role-Based Access Control, Custody Handshake, Straight-Line Depreciation.

Chapter One: Introduction

1.1 Background of the Study

Higher educational institutions serve as complex organizational environments where thousands of physical, educational, electronic, computational, and infrastructural assets are continuously acquired, distributed, utilized, and maintained. Debre Berhan University (DBU), located in Debre Berhan, Amhara Region, Ethiopia, has experienced rapid structural expansion since its establishment. The university currently encompasses diverse academic faculties—including the College of Computing and Informatics, Faculty of Technology, Faculty of Business and Economics, and specialized branch campuses such as the Asrat Weldyes Campus—alongside vital central directorates such as the ICT Directorate and Administration Office.

As institutional capital investments in physical assets expand, the governance and accounting of university property become indispensable to academic success and fiscal integrity. In large universities, physical property spans high-value IT workstations, server infrastructure, audiovisual laboratory instruments, ergonomic classroom furniture, specialized medical tools, and institutional utility vehicles. Traditionally, physical property administration at Debre Berhan University depended primarily on paper-based ledger registers, handwritten store issue vouchers (Model 19, Model 20, and Model 22 vouchers), and decentralized spreadsheet records maintained across individual academic departments.

While paper-based systems historically provided basic audit trails for small-scale operations, their reliance on manual record keeping creates immense friction in a multi-campus university setting. Computerized asset tracking platforms centralize property data into a unified, accessible, and mathematically verifiable database. By associating physical items with machine-readable optical markers—such as two-dimensional Quick Response (QR) codes and linear barcodes—institutions eliminate human recording errors, enable instantaneous custody verification, automate straight-line capital depreciation, and establish immutable audit logging across the entire asset lifecycle.

1.2 Problem Statement

An exhaustive empirical analysis of the property administration workflow at Debre Berhan University reveals severe structural, operational, and financial vulnerabilities inherent in the existing manual and fragmented paradigm:

  • Difficulty Tracking Assets and Location Blindness: Due to decentralized record keeping, property administration officers struggle to maintain real-time visibility over the exact campus, building, room number, or departmental custody of thousands of distributed assets.
  • Asset Assignment and Custody Deniability: When property is issued to staff members or departments, conventional paper vouchers often fail to establish unambiguous, reciprocal accountability. Staff members can dispute custody of unreturned or damaged equipment due to lost paper slips or illegible handwritten signatures, fostering accountability voids.
  • Maintenance and Defect Tracking Deficits: Defective, malfunctioning, or damaged assets are frequently returned to central stores without systematic diagnostic documentation. Maintenance technicians lack a centralized digital queue to triage tickets, track spare-part expenditures, monitor repair progression, or record labor costs. Consequently, equipment sits dormant for prolonged intervals without scheduled repair.
  • Labor-Intensive Annual Physical Inventory Verification: Annual stocktaking and physical verification require teams of inventory personnel to manually cross-reference paper ledgers room by room. This process requires weeks of labor, is acutely susceptible to manual transcription mistakes, and offers no mechanism to instantly flag cross-departmental asset transfers or prevent duplicate counting.
  • Absence of Systematic Financial and Capital Depreciation Tracking: Institutional accounting standards require higher education institutions to maintain accurate balance sheet valuations reflecting historical acquisition costs, accumulated depreciation, salvage value floors, and current net book values. Under manual methods, calculating straight-line amortization across thousands of heterogeneous assets is practically impossible, obscuring institutional net worth and budget planning.
  • Lack of Real-Time Public Verification and Campus Security Risks: Campus security guards at university gates and internal auditors lack any automated tool to verify whether an asset carried across campus gates is legitimately assigned to the possessor, legitimately transferred, or unauthorizedly removed.

1.3 General Objective

The general objective of this project is to analyze, design, develop, implement, and rigorously verify a secure, centralized, web-based, QR-enabled DBU Asset Tracking System to automate institutional property cataloging, custody lifecycle handshakes, maintenance ticketing, physical inventory audits, and financial depreciation tracking at Debre Berhan University.

1.4 Specific Objectives

To realize the general objective, the project formulated the following concrete software engineering objectives:

  • To inspect and model the organizational hierarchy of Debre Berhan University, incorporating faculties, colleges, academic departments, administrative directorates, and distinct stakeholder roles into a unified relational database schema.
  • To design and implement a robust Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) security architecture supporting seven distinct institutional roles with Edge middleware route protection and automated dashboard redirection.
  • To develop a Dynamic Asset Registration and Form Builder engine that allows administrators to dynamically configure custom technical specification fields per asset type while enforcing uniqueness constraints on asset codes and serial numbers.
  • To engineer an automated QR Code and Code 39 Barcode generation engine that produces high-resolution, printable asset tags and associates each physical asset with a non-guessable, privacy-safe publicId.
  • To implement a deterministic Two-Phase Custody Handshake workflow requiring staff assignees to formally accept or reject asset assignments with mandatory audit justifications before custody is transferred.
  • To construct a privacy-preserving Zero-Authentication Public QR Verification interface that allows campus visitors and security guards to verify asset authenticity via mobile cameras while cryptographically suppressing all sensitive financial, procurement, and personal identity records.
  • To build automated maintenance, physical inventory verification, and straight-line capital depreciation modules supporting immutable transaction audit logging, replacement priority scoring, and executive financial balance sheet analytics.

1.5 Significance of the Study

The implementation of the DBU Asset Tracking System delivers measurable strategic, financial, and operational benefits across diverse university stakeholders:

  • University Administration & Directorate: Provides executive visibility into institutional capital investments, aggregate asset values, departmental budget allocations, and equipment replacement forecasts through real-time dashboards.
  • Property Administration Officers (PAOs): Eliminates manual paper vouchers, accelerates asset registration, automates asset tag printing, and provides centralized control over custody handshakes and inter-departmental transfers.
  • Department Heads: Grants oversight over departmental equipment, facilitates structured staff asset request reviews, and provides an administrative appeal mechanism to dispute resource allocation.
  • Academic & Administrative Staff: Protects staff members from false custody claims by providing a personal custody portal where assignments must be explicitly confirmed, defects can be reported, and returns are formally logged.
  • Maintenance Technicians: Centralizes repair ticket triage, prioritizes urgent maintenance work orders, and logs cumulative labor and spare-part expenses directly into asset lifecycle histories.
  • Inventory Personnel & Internal Auditors: Accelerates annual physical inventory audits via mobile QR scanning, prevents duplicate counts, detects departmental mismatches in real time, and maintains tamper-evident audit logs.
  • Campus Security Personnel: Enables security officers at university access gates to instantly scan asset QR codes on mobile devices to confirm legitimate possession and authorization status without requiring system login credentials.

1.6 Scope of the System

The implemented scope of the DBU Asset Tracking System is strictly defined by the verified capabilities implemented in the application source code:

  • Functional Scope: User authentication; encrypted JWT session handling; role-based access control across 7 stakeholder roles; organizational unit management (faculties, departments, colleges); dynamic asset registration with custom technical specifications; QR code and Code 39 barcode generation; printable label creation; two-phase assignment acceptance and rejection; asset returns with automatic maintenance ticket generation for damaged goods; inter-departmental transfer requests and approvals; maintenance ticket lifecycle triage; physical inventory session creation and mobile QR scanning; internal transaction audit logging; straight-line financial depreciation and net book value calculation; replacement scoring; and staff asset requests and property appeals.
  • Organizational Scope: Tailored specifically to the administrative and academic hierarchy of Debre Berhan University, covering academic colleges (such as Computing and Informatics), technical faculties, branch campuses, administrative offices, and central property directorates.
  • Technical Scope: A cloud-native web platform accessible across modern desktop, laptop, tablet, and mobile browsers without requiring native operating system installation.

1.7 Limitations of the System

To preserve academic integrity, genuine operational limitations of the implemented system are explicitly documented:

  • Network Connectivity Dependency: Because the system utilizes a cloud-native architecture connecting Next.js server actions to a remote Neon Serverless PostgreSQL database over secure TLS tunnels, active internet or university intranet connectivity is mandatory for core transactions. Offline synchronization is not currently implemented.
  • Optical Scanning Constraints: QR code and barcode scanning in the field rely on standard device cameras via the open-source jsQR software library. In low-light environments, damaged physical tags or low-resolution camera hardware can impact optical recognition speed compared to dedicated industrial laser scanners.
  • Absence of Active RFID Hardware Integration: The system utilizes passive optical identification (QR/barcodes). Real-time automated spatial tracking via active Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) transponders or ceiling-mounted IoT sensor gates is outside the current implementation scope.

3.2 System Architecture

The DBU Asset Tracking System is engineered according to a modern, decoupled, multi-tier software architecture leveraging the Next.js 14 App Router framework. Figure 3.2 illustrates the layered component structure and data flow.

Figure 3.2: System Architecture Diagram – Layered Component Structure

The architecture comprises four distinct logical tiers:

  • 1. Presentation Tier (Client & Server Components): Built using React 18, React Server Components (RSC), and Tailwind CSS. The user interface delivers seven dedicated dashboard panels tailored to each stakeholder role. Client-side components handle dynamic interactions—such as Recharts interactive financial charts, HTML5 video camera QR scanning via jsQR, dynamic form builder drag-and-drop interfaces, and client-side label printing. Server components fetch pre-rendered markup directly on the server, minimizing client JavaScript bundle size.
  • 2. Application Routing & Security Tier: Governed by the Next.js 14 App Router, NextAuth.js (v5), and Edge middleware (middleware.ts). Incoming HTTP requests are intercepted at the edge to verify encrypted JWT session cookies (authjs.session-token). The middleware enforces strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), automatically routing users to their authorized dashboards while whitelisting the public verification route (/asset/verify/[publicId]). Server actions ("use server") provide type-safe RPC endpoints that mutate state and trigger atomic path revalidation (revalidatePath).
  • 3. Domain Business Logic Tier: Implemented as specialized TypeScript domain services (src/services). This tier encapsulates core institutional business rules, including: (a) dynamic custom field validation in asset.ts; (b) two-phase custody handshake state machines in assignment.ts and return.ts; (c) straight-line capital depreciation and replacement priority algorithms in financials.ts and valuation.ts; (d) physical inventory reconciliation in inventory.ts; (e) privacy-safe name masking and rate limiting in public-asset.ts and privacy.ts; and (f) immutable audit trail creation in audit.ts.
  • 4. Data Access & Persistence Tier: Comprises Prisma ORM v7 client, connection pooling adapters (@prisma/adapter-pg), and a cloud-native PostgreSQL relational database hosted on Neon. The Prisma client translates TypeScript queries into optimized SQL, executes multi-table atomic transactions via the $transaction API, enforces foreign key relational constraints, and manages schema migrations.

4.2 Authentication and Role-Based Authorization Implementation

Authentication is implemented using NextAuth.js v5 configured with a custom Credentials Provider (src/auth.ts). User passwords are encrypted using bcryptjs with a salt work factor of 10. During authentication, the system supports lookup by either email address or institutional username. Upon successful credential validation, the jwt callback injects the user's unique ID, assigned role (RoleName), and department ID into an encrypted JSON Web Token stored in an HTTP-only, SameSite-protected cookie named authjs.session-token.

Authorization is enforced at the edge via Next.js middleware (src/middleware.ts). The middleware intercepts all incoming requests and verifies whether the user possesses an active session. Unauthenticated requests targeting protected routes are immediately redirected to /login via HTTP 307 redirects. For authenticated users, the middleware evaluates role permissions against targeted route segments: requests to /admin/* require SYSTEM_ADMINISTRATOR; requests to /pao/* require PROPERTY_ADMINISTRATION_OFFICER; requests to /head/* require DEPARTMENT_HEAD; requests to /staff/* require STAFF_MEMBER; requests to /tech/* require MAINTENANCE_TECHNICIAN; requests to /auditor/* require INTERNAL_AUDITOR; and requests to /inventory/* require INVENTORY_PERSON. When an authenticated user visits the root path (/), the middleware automatically resolves their role and redirects them to their designated dashboard. Crucially, the public verification route (/asset/verify/*) is explicitly whitelisted to permit instant zero-authentication verification by campus security and visitors.

4.3 User, Organizational Unit, and Role Management

User management is centralized under the System Administrator console (src/app/(dashboard)/admin/users). Administrators can provision new user accounts, designate institutional roles, and bind users to specific organizational units. To support Debre Berhan University's administrative hierarchy, the database models Faculty records (e.g., Faculty of Technology, Faculty of Business and Economics) linked to child OrganizationalUnit records (e.g., Software Engineering, Computer Science, Accounting) supporting parent-child self-referential relations. When a user account is first initialized with a temporary password, the mustChangePassword flag is set to true, causing the middleware to intercept and force redirection to /profile/security until a secure personal password is established.

4.4 Dynamic Asset Registration & Specification Engine

Asset registration (implemented in src/services/asset.ts and src/components/asset-registration-client.tsx) handles both standard property accounting fields and dynamically defined technical specifications. Core attributes include: asset name, unique asset code, unique serial number, description, category, asset type, department, campus, building, room number, funding source (GOVERNMENT_BUDGET, UNIVERSITY_INTERNAL_BUDGET, RESEARCH_GRANT, DONATION), purchase date, purchase cost, expected lifecycle years, and salvage value.

To accommodate diverse asset types without altering the underlying database schema, the system implements a Dynamic Form Builder (AssetTypeField, RegistrationField, and AssetFieldValue). Administrators can configure custom fields (e.g., RAM capacity, CPU model, Vehicle Engine Number, Chassis Number) per asset type, specifying field types (TEXT, NUMBER, DECIMAL, DATE, DROPDOWN), default values, validation ranges, and mandatory flags. During asset creation, registerAsset dynamically queries active configurations for the selected asset type, validates submitted values against configured bounds, and atomically persists them in the AssetFieldValue table within a single transaction.

4.5 Asset Assignment, Two-Phase Handshake, and Return Workflows

The asset custody workflow implements a deterministic state machine designed to eliminate custody deniability. In src/services/assignment.ts, the assignAsset function creates an Assignment record with status PENDING_ACCEPTANCE and transitions the asset status from ACTIVE to PENDING_ASSIGNMENT. The transaction dispatches an in-app notification to the designated staff member. While pending, the asset cannot be assigned to any other user or transferred.

The staff member logs into their dashboard, inspects the physical equipment, and chooses between two reciprocal actions:

  • Acceptance: Calling acceptAssignment verifies that the caller matches assignedToUserId. It atomically updates the assignment status to ACCEPTED, records the timestamp in acceptedAt, transitions the asset status to ASSIGNED, logs an audit entry, and notifies the PAO.
  • Rejection: Calling rejectAssignment requires a mandatory rejection explanation string. The function updates the assignment status to REJECTED, records rejectionReason, reverts the asset status back to ACTIVE, and alerts the PAO to reallocate the asset.

Asset returns (src/services/return.ts) allow property officers to log equipment check-in. The return record captures conditionAtReturn (GOOD vs. DAMAGED). If marked DAMAGED, the transaction automatically generates a maintenance ticket and transitions the asset to UNDER_MAINTENANCE. Inter-departmental transfers (src/services/transfer.ts) allow department heads to request asset relocations, requiring formal approval by a PAO before custody and department bindings are updated.

4.6 QR Code, Barcode Engineering, and Public Verification

Optical identification is engineered through two complementary standards:

  • QR Code Generation: Utilizing the qrcode package, the system generates high-density two-dimensional QR codes. Crucially, the QR code encodes a secure public verification URL pointing to the university domain: https://dbu-asset-tracking.vercel.app/asset/verify/[publicId]. The encoded target uses the asset's random publicId UUID, completely decoupling public optical scanning from the internal database primary key.
  • Native Code 39 Barcode Generator: To ensure zero-dependency client and server rendering of linear barcodes, the system implements a native SVG generator (generateBarcodeSvg in asset-registration-client.tsx and asset-details-client.tsx). The algorithm encodes alphanumeric asset codes into standardized Code 39 patterns using 9 binary elements (narrow and wide bars/spaces) flanked by start/stop asterisks, rendering crisp vector barcodes printable on standard label stock.
  • Zero-Authentication Public Verification (src/services/public-asset.ts): The endpoint /asset/verify/[publicId] allows any visitor, auditor, or security guard to verify asset authenticity without logging in. To protect institutional confidentiality, the query enforces rigorous privacy guarantees: (1) sensitive financial fields (purchase cost, salvage value, annual depreciation, book value) are strictly excluded from the database projection; (2) procurement details (supplier names, invoices) are omitted; (3) internal UUID primary keys are suppressed; and (4) custodian names are cryptographically masked via maskPersonName (e.g., "Abreham Kebede" is rendered as "Abreham K."). Furthermore, IP-based rate limiting (checkVerificationRateLimit) restricts lookups to 30 requests per minute per IP address to prevent automated harvesting.

4.7 Maintenance Management & Defect Triage

Maintenance operations (src/services/maintenance.ts) provide end-to-end defect tracking. Tickets are created either through explicit staff reports or automatically via damaged return check-ins. Tickets include fault descriptions and priority levels (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH). Technicians review open tickets on their dashboard (/tech/dashboard), accept assignments (setting status to IN_PROGRESS), and complete repairs by submitting labor and spare-part expenses (setting status to COMPLETED). Upon completion, the system transitions the asset status back to ACTIVE and updates cumulative maintenance expenditures.

4.8 Physical Inventory Sessions & Internal Audit Log Engine

Physical inventory verification (src/services/inventory.ts) streamlines annual university stocktaking. A Property Administration Officer initializes an InventorySession for a specific department and assigns it to a designated InventoryPerson. The inventory officer accesses the mobile-optimized interface (/inventory/[id]) and scans physical asset QR codes or barcodes using their smartphone camera.

The scanning service enforces rigorous anti-tamper validations: (1) it verifies that the scanned asset exists and is active; (2) it checks department affinity—if an asset registered to "Software Engineering" is scanned during an "Accounting" session, the system rejects the scan and alerts the user of a cross-department mismatch; and (3) it checks the composite unique index on [inventorySessionId, assetId]—if an asset has already been counted in the active session, the system rejects the duplicate scan. Upon session conclusion, unverified items are automatically flagged as missing.

4.9 Financial Accounting, Depreciation & Replacement Scoring

Financial asset accounting (src/services/financials.ts and src/services/valuation.ts) implements standardized straight-line capital depreciation. The mathematical model evaluates metrics dynamically as of any calculation date:

  • Depreciable Base: Calculated as acquisition cost minus salvage value: $$\text{Depreciable Base} = \max(0, \text{Purchase Cost} - \text{Salvage Value})$$
  • Amortization Granularity: Evaluates total useful life in months ($\text{Useful Life Months} = \text{Useful Life Years} \times 12$). Elapsed operational time is computed in month units and clamped to $[0, \text{Useful Life Months}]$: $$\text{Clamped Months} = \min(\max(0, \text{Elapsed Months}), \text{Useful Life Months})$$
  • Monthly & Accumulated Depreciation: Monthly depreciation is defined as: $$\text{Monthly Depreciation} = \frac{\text{Depreciable Base}}{\text{Useful Life Months}}$$ $$\text{Accumulated Depreciation} = \min(\text{Depreciable Base}, \text{Monthly Depreciation} \times \text{Clamped Months})$$
  • Current Book Value: Strictly floored at the salvage value: $$\text{Current Book Value} = \max(\text{Salvage Value}, \text{Purchase Cost} - \text{Accumulated Depreciation})$$
  • Replacement Priority Score (0–100): Evaluates whether an asset should be kept, monitored, or replaced based on a weighted composite formula: (1) Age factor (max 30 points); (2) Accumulated depreciation ratio (max 20 points); (3) Cumulative repair-to-cost ratio (max 25 points); and (4) Operational condition/status factor (max 25 points). Scores $\ge 75$ generate a critical replacement recommendation.

4.10 Multi-Tiered Asset Requests, Notifications, and Appeals

The resource allocation workflow (src/services/asset-request.ts and src/services/appeal.ts) implements a formal two-tiered requisition hierarchy. Staff members submit asset requests specifying item categories, quantities, and justifications (status: PENDING_DEPARTMENT_HEAD). The Department Head reviews pending departmental requests, approving or rejecting them. Approved requests advance to the Property Administration Office (status: APPROVED_BY_DEPARTMENT_HEAD). A PAO fulfills the request by linking an available physical asset and initiating the assignment handshake, or rejects the request with written remarks. If an urgent request is denied, the Department Head can submit a formal PropertyAppeal to property management, complete with supporting evidence and dispute tracking.

Property Administration Officers now maintain instantaneous visibility over the exact physical location, operational condition, and custodian bindings of every registered capital asset across all academic faculties.

7.2 Real-Time Custody Governance and Financial Transparency

A central innovation of the system is the Two-Phase Custody Handshake Engine. Historically, custody disputes arose because staff members could claim that equipment listed on store issue vouchers was never received or was delivered in damaged condition. Under the implemented system, physical custody is legally and digitally indeterminate until the recipient logs into the platform, inspects the asset, and formally triggers acceptAssignment. Conversely, if an item is defective or assigned erroneously, the staff member rejects custody with a mandatory audit justification, instantly alerting administrators.

From an institutional accounting standpoint, the dynamic Straight-Line Depreciation Engine bridges the historical divide between physical property administration and capital balance sheet accounting. Rather than calculating depreciation intermittently using manual estimations, the system computes elapsed amortization, monthly depreciation, and carrying book values in real time based on active calendar elapsed months. The replacement scoring algorithm provides empirical decision support, helping university leadership prioritize equipment replacement before cumulative repair expenses exceed asset value.

7.3 Physical Verification Efficiency & QR Tag Security

The physical inventory verification subsystem reduces annual stocktaking labor from several weeks of manual recording to rapid mobile camera scans. By enforcing cross-departmental mismatch checks and composite unique constraints against duplicate counts, the system prevents common auditing errors.

Furthermore, the Zero-Authentication Public QR Verification Interface resolves gate security vulnerabilities. Campus security personnel can instantly scan an asset's optical tag with standard mobile cameras to verify legitimate registration and custody status. The implementation of selective column projection, cryptographic name masking, and IP rate limiting guarantees that public verification never compromises institutional confidentiality or leaks personal custodian data.

7.4 Architectural Trade-Offs and Lessons Learned

The choice of a serverless full-stack architecture utilizing Next.js 14, Prisma ORM, and Neon Serverless PostgreSQL offered substantial engineering advantages, including rapid development velocity, unified TypeScript type safety, and zero server maintenance overhead. However, it also introduced specific architectural trade-offs:

  • Connection Pooling in Serverless Environments: Transient serverless functions can rapidly exhaust database connections during concurrent bursts. Integrating @prisma/adapter-pg with Neon's PgBouncer pooler successfully mitigated connection spikes, highlighting the necessity of connection multiplexing in serverless database deployments.
  • Optical Scanning vs. Industrial Hardware: While software-based optical decoding via jsQR eliminates expensive dedicated hardware, it remains dependent on environmental lighting and camera focus. Implementing fallback search by asset code ensured reliable operation across all devices.

B.2 Mathematical Valuation Engine Proof

Given an asset with acquisition cost $C = 85,000\text{ ETB}$, estimated salvage value $S = 5,000\text{ ETB}$, and expected operational life $L = 5\text{ years}$ ($60\text{ months}$), the straight-line parameters are derived as follows:

1. Depreciable Base ($B$): $$B = C - S = 85,000 - 5,000 = 80,000\text{ ETB}$$

2. Monthly Amortization Rate ($R_m$): $$R_m = \frac{B}{L \times 12} = \frac{80,000}{60} = 1,333.333...\text{ ETB/month}$$

3. Accumulated Depreciation at $M = 24\text{ months}$: $$D_{24} = \min(B, R_m \times 24) = 1,333.333... \times 24 = 32,000.00\text{ ETB}$$

4. Carrying Book Value ($V_{24}$): $$V_{24} = \max(S, C - D_{24}) = \max(5,000, 85,000 - 32,000) = 53,000.00\text{ ETB}$$

5. Salvage Value Protection at $M \ge 60\text{ months}$: $$\lim_{M \to \infty} V_M = \max(5,000, 85,000 - \min(80,000, R_m \times M)) = 5,000.00\text{ ETB}$$

The algorithm guarantees that an asset's book value never drops below its statutory salvage floor, preserving institutional accounting compliance under all operating horizons.
